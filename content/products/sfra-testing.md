# SFRA Testing — Sweep Frequency Response Analysis

## Sweep Frequency Response Analysis (SFRA) for Power Transformers

SFRA (Sweep Frequency Response Analysis) is the most sensitive technique available for detecting mechanical deformation of power transformer windings and core — damage that can occur during short-circuit events, heavy through-fault currents, transportation incidents, or seismic events. Green Watt supplies SFRA test systems that allow asset managers to quickly and confidently assess whether a transformer's internal geometry has changed, without opening the tank.

## How SFRA Works

The SFRA instrument injects a swept-frequency low-voltage signal (typically 20 Hz to 2 MHz) into one terminal of a transformer winding and measures the response at another terminal. The resulting frequency response curve is a precise electromagnetic "fingerprint" of the winding geometry, core position, and inter-winding capacitances. By comparing the current fingerprint against a baseline (factory fingerprint or previous field measurement), even small changes in winding geometry — such as axial displacement, radial deformation, or turn-to-turn shorts — produce a detectable deviation.

## Key Features

- **Wide frequency sweep** — 20 Hz to 2 MHz for full winding, core, and bushing coverage
- **High dynamic range** — up to 100 dB dynamic range for detecting subtle deformation signatures
- **Fast measurement** — complete sweep in under 1 minute per winding terminal pair
- **Standard test connections** — End-to-End Open Circuit, End-to-End Short Circuit, Capacitive Inter-Winding, and inductive measurements per IEC 60076-18 / CIGRE TB342
- **Automatic comparison** — overlay of current measurement against stored baseline with deviation reporting
- **Fingerprint database** — stores multiple baselines per transformer for trend analysis
- **PC software** — advanced analysis tools including phase angle plots, deviation statistics, and automated assessment
- **Lightweight portable unit** — calibrated signal generator and acquisition in a single carry case

## Applications

- Post-short-circuit assessment before returning a transformer to service
- Pre- and post-transportation fingerprinting for new and repaired transformers
- Routine asset condition assessment as part of transformer life management
- Post-seismic event verification
- Ranking transformers for prioritised refurbishment or replacement

## Compliance

Test procedures follow IEC 60076-18, CIGRE Technical Brochure 342, and IEEE C57.149 guidelines for SFRA on power transformers.
